# FTRA Monetization Strategies

Upfront caveat: actual traffic numbers for fullthairulesaustralia.com aren't known here — treat the pricing tiers below as starting frames to validate against real numbers, not prescriptions. The "only ranking platform in Australia" position is what makes most of these viable — defensibility comes from being the canonical source, not from volume alone.

---

## 1. Combat sports insurance — direct partnership (rebuilt)

The wedge the ex-partner was clipping. Cut the middleman by going direct to one or two insurers who underwrite combat sports cover (gym liability, fighter accident, event public liability). Offer them an **exclusive Australian Muay Thai sponsorship** of the rankings — logo on division pages, "Official Insurance Partner of FTRA Rankings," referral links from every fighter and gym profile.

- **Who pays:** insurer, annual sponsorship + per-lead referral fee.
- **Frame:** $25–60k/yr exclusive, plus $20–80 per qualified gym/fighter lead.
- **Why it works:** rankings are *the* signal of legitimacy in the sport — gyms and promoters checking rankings are the exact buyers insurers want.
- **Risk:** thin market in AU; may only be 2–3 viable insurers, so leverage is finite.

## 2. Verified Fighter Profiles (freemium → paid)

Free basic profile, paid tier (~$49–99/yr) unlocks: verified badge, custom banner, fight footage embeds, sponsor logos, direct booking/contact form, social links, Instagram-style highlight reel.

- **Who pays:** fighters (especially pros and rising amateurs trying to attract promoters).
- **Why it works:** rankings drive the traffic; fighters near the top have real incentive to convert profile views into sponsorships, fight offers, gym sign-ups.
- **Risk:** AU pro Muay Thai roster is small — maybe a few hundred genuinely active fighters. Volume play is weak; price has to be justified by outcomes.

## 3. Gym / Stable subscriptions

Tiered SaaS for gyms: list the gym, claim a stable page, manage their fighters' linked profiles, post events, push their ranked fighters in a "Featured Stables" carousel.

- **Tiers:** Free (listing only) / Pro ~$39/mo (stable page + analytics) / Elite ~$99/mo (homepage placement, lead capture form, email capture).
- **Why it works:** gyms compete intensely for new students. Being able to say "we have 4 fighters in the FTRA top 10" is direct marketing leverage — and you let them showcase it.
- **Risk:** churn on small gyms; needs a real product (CMS, analytics) not just a static page.

## 4. Event listings + ticket affiliate

Promoters post events free in a basic feed; paid placement ($150–500/event) for featured slots, homepage banner, "Sanctioned Event" badge, integrated countdowns on ranked-fighter profiles fighting on the card. Layer on a ticketing affiliate (Humanitix, Eventbrite, Oztix) — 2–5% on tickets sold through your link.

- **Why it works:** every promoter wants visibility; every ranked fighter on a card is free promo for the event.
- **Bonus:** you become the de facto Australian Muay Thai event calendar, which compounds SEO.

## 5. Sanctioning / data licensing to commissions and feds

License the ranking data feed to state combat sports authorities (CSA NSW, VIC PCSAA, QLD OLGR), national federations, and international bodies (WBC Muay Thai, WMC, IFMA).

- **Model:** annual licensing fee + co-branding. $5–25k per body.
- **Why it works:** state authorities have a regulatory interest in fighter records; feds want a credible AU pipeline. You're the only one already maintaining the dataset.
- **Risk:** sales cycle is long and political; needs at least one anchor tenant to legitimize.

## 6. Title sponsorship of divisions

Sell each of the 24 weight divisions to a sponsor — "The [Brand] Welterweight Rankings." Tiered by division popularity (welterweight/lightweight male go for more than fly).

- **Model:** $3–15k/yr per division × 24 = a meaningful floor if even half sell. Bundle with banner placement on division pages.
- **Buyers:** gear brands (Fairtex, Yokkao, InFightStyle, Boon AU distributors), supplements, recovery products, local Muay Thai shorts brands.
- **Why it works:** division pages are high-intent — anyone reading them is a serious enthusiast.

## 7. Targeted display + affiliate ads

Direct-sold over programmatic (programmatic CPMs in this niche are rubbish). Combat sports gear, supplements, gyms — affiliate links inside fighter profiles ("[Fighter] trains in [Gear Brand] gloves → buy here").

- **Model:** flat monthly placements ($300–2k/mo) for direct deals; 5–15% affiliate on gear via Awin/Commission Factory or direct.
- **Why it works:** audience is ultra-niche and high-intent — gear buyers in particular convert well.
- **Risk:** don't junk up the cinematic design; one wrong banner and the whole aesthetic collapses.

## 8. Fight matchmaking / "Find me an opponent" service

Promoters and fighters use FTRA to match fights at weight + skill level. Charge promoters per successful match, or sell as a feature inside a Pro Promoter subscription.

- **Model:** $50–200 per match facilitated, or $99/mo unlimited for promoters.
- **Why it works:** matchmaking is the single hardest, most time-consuming job in promoting Muay Thai. You already have the ranking data — turning it into a tool is a small product leap with high pain-point value.

## 9. Merch + storefront-as-a-service

Two layers:

- **a)** FTRA-branded merch (rankings tees, championship-style belts for each #1, "Top 10" wall plaques fighters can buy as keepsakes).
- **b)** Per-fighter storefront via Printful/Printify integration — fighters sell their own merch off their profile, FTRA takes 15–25%.
- **Why it works:** ranked fighters have small but engaged followings; giving them a one-click merch storefront tied to their ranking is a no-brainer for them.
- **Risk:** ops overhead unless you stay print-on-demand only.

## 10. Picks / predictions game with sponsorship layer

Free fantasy/prediction game for upcoming AU Muay Thai cards — users predict winners, leaderboard, weekly prizes. Monetized via:

- A title sponsor for the game ("[Brand] Fight Picks").
- Email capture (now you own the audience, not just a session).
- Prize fulfilment from gear sponsors (in-kind, no cash cost to you).
- **Why it works:** turns one-time ranking-checkers into recurring weekly visitors. Engagement compounds the value of every other revenue line above.
- **Note on betting affiliate:** avoid wagering affiliates in AU — combat sports + gambling is a regulatory/PR hornet's nest, and several states restrict it. Predictions-for-prizes is the safer adjacent play.

## Suggested sequencing

Stacked by effort vs. revenue per hour invested:

1. **Insurance partnership (#1)** — one phone call could replace what was previously being skimmed.
2. **Division sponsorships (#6)** — sellable today with the existing site, no product build.
3. **Event listings + ticketing affiliate (#4)** — light build, recurring inventory.
4. **Verified Fighter Profiles (#2)** + **Gym subscriptions (#3)** — these are the SaaS layer and need real product work.
5. Everything else after the base is in.

The combo of #1, #4, and #6 is probably ~70% of realistic year-one revenue with ~20% of the build effort.

## Assumptions & uncertainties

- **Traffic:** no actual numbers available here. Pricing tiers above assume "small but enthusiast-dense" traffic typical of niche combat sports sites — if MAU is <2k some of these (especially #7 programmatic) need to be scaled down or skipped. If higher, prices float up.
- **Ex-partner / insurance backstory:** taken at face value — no independent confirmation of the past arrangement or the insurer mix in AU.
- **Pricing ranges:** all illustrative starting points based on comparable niche-sports-vertical economics, not pulled from FTRA-specific data. Validate with one or two real conversations before committing.
- **Regulatory:** state combat sports commissions in AU vary — some monetization angles (especially #5 and anything brushing #10/gambling) need legal review per state. Don't act on those without checking.
- **Roster size:** assumes the AU active pro Muay Thai roster is in the low hundreds. If materially larger, #2 scales better than framed.
